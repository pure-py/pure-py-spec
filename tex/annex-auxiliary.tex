\section{Syntactic analyses}
\label{sec:mf-syntactic}

The analyses $\assignsS$, $\binds$, $\capturesS$ and $\capturesE$ introduced in \secref{wf-aux} are
defined by their principal cases; the remaining cases are stated with each.

\begin{auxfunction}{\assignsS(s)}{set of variables}{aux:assigns}
The variables assigned anywhere in $s$, not descending into nested function definitions, which have their
own scope. It is empty on the statements not shown and is the union over the branch blocks of a
conditional. It extends by union from statements to blocks, with the singleton block identified with its
statement, and on a module body $\seq{\iota}\;\seq{s}$ acts on $\seq{s}$.
\begin{align*}
\input{functions/assigns}
\end{align*}
\end{auxfunction}

\begin{auxfunction}{\binds(p)}{set of variables}{aux:binds}
The variables introduced by pattern $p$. On the cases not shown it recurses structurally, taking the union
over the immediate subpatterns.
\begin{align*}
\input{functions/binds}
\end{align*}
\end{auxfunction}

\begin{auxfunction}{\capturesS(s)}{set of variables}{aux:captures}
The variables of the enclosing scope captured by closures (lambdas or nested function definitions) within
$s$. On the cases not shown it takes the union of $\capturesE$ over the subexpressions of $s$ and of
$\capturesS$ over its blocks, and extends to blocks and module bodies as $\assignsS$ does.
\begin{align*}
\input{functions/captures}
\end{align*}
\end{auxfunction}

\begin{auxfunction}{\capturesE(e)}{set of variables}{aux:captures-e}
The variables of the enclosing scope captured by closures within expression $e$. On the cases not shown it
recurses structurally, taking the union over the immediate subexpressions.
\begin{align*}
\input{functions/captures-e}
\end{align*}
\end{auxfunction}

\begin{auxfunction}{\fvS(b), \fvE(e)}{set of variables}{aux:fv}
The free variables of a block and of an expression, defined in the usual way.
\end{auxfunction}

\section{Values}
\label{sec:mf-values}

The functions on values used by the evaluation rules. The operators, structural equality and membership
have the meanings they have in Python and yield $\aborts{\kappa}$ where Python raises. All three are
undefined where no case applies, and where one of them is undefined the expression has no derivation,
which is where \PurePy declines to give the operation the meaning Python gives it; a non-Boolean test is
the same case.

\begin{auxfunction}{\eqOp(v, v')}{Boolean}{aux:eq}
Structural equality: literals by value, lists and tuples elementwise, dictionaries by keys and
entries, and objects by class and then by fields. \pyinline{None} compares with any value;
otherwise equality is undefined between values of different kinds. Sequences are compared from the
first element and comparison stops at the first unequal one. Because equality is partial, this order is
observable: an undefined comparison later in a sequence does not make the whole undefined. The same
holds of $\eqEntries$ and $\memOp$ below.
\begin{align*}
\input{functions/eq}
\end{align*}
\end{auxfunction}

\begin{auxfunction}{\eqEntries(\delta, \delta')}{Boolean}{aux:eq-entries}
Whether each entry of $\delta$ has an equal value under its key in $\delta'$, taking the entries in
order and stopping at the first unequal one; used by $\eqOp$ on dictionaries with the same keys.
\begin{align*}

\end{auxfunction}
