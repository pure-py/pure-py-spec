\section{Graduality}
\label{sec:graduality}

\figref{graduality-precision} gives the gradual types. Define $\TypeSet$ to be the set of
types, as given in \specification, and $\GTypeSet$ to be the set
of gradual types; $\sigma$ and $\tau$ range over $\TypeSet$, and $\gty{\sigma}$ and $\gty{\tau}$ over
$\GTypeSet$. Concretisation and abstraction are from \citet{garcia2016}; precision is from
\citet{siek2015}.

\input{spec/fig/graduality-precision}

\begin{definition}[Precision]
\label{def:precision}
Precision $\precTy$, a partial order on gradual types, is defined in \figref{graduality-precision}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Discreteness of static types]
\label{lem:static-discrete}
If $\sigma \precTy \tau$ then $\sigma = \tau$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{definition}[Concretisation]
\label{def:concretisation}
Concretisation $\gamma : \GTypeSet \to \powersetplus{\TypeSet}$ is defined in \figref{graduality-concretisation}.
\end{definition}

\input{spec/fig/graduality-concretisation}

\begin{lemma}[Preservation and reflection of precision]
\label{lem:precision-concretisation}
$\gty{\sigma} \precTy \gty{\tau}$ if and only if $\gamma(\gty{\sigma}) \subseteq \gamma(\gty{\tau})$.
\end{lemma}

A gradual type $\gty{\tau}$ \emph{approximates} a set $S$ of types when $S \subseteq \gamma(\gty{\tau})$;
$\tyAny$ approximates every set of types. A least approximant is a best abstraction in the sense
of \citet{cousot1977}. Preservation is monotonicity of concretisation. Reflection forces every
approximant of $S$ to lie above the join of $S$, so the join is the least approximant; without
reflection it would approximate $S$ but need not be least.

\begin{lemma}[Least approximant]
\label{lem:least-approximant}
For every nonempty set $S$ of types, the least upper bound $\bigsqcup S$ under $\precTy$ exists and
is the least $\gty{\tau}$ that approximates $S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\gamma(\sigma) = \set{\sigma}$ for every type $\sigma$, approximating $S$ is the same as
bounding $S$ above under $\precTy$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:precision-concretisation}). The join of two
gradual types (\figref{graduality-join}) is their least upper bound, by induction on the pair, so
every finite nonempty set of gradual types has a least upper bound, by induction on its size. Fix
$\sigma \in S$. The gradual types above $\sigma$ are obtained by replacing subterms of $\sigma$ with
$\tyAny$, so there are finitely many; the joins $\bigsqcup F$ for finite $F \subseteq S$ containing
$\sigma$ lie among them and increase with $F$, so some $\bigsqcup F$ is maximal. Then
$\tau \precTy \bigsqcup (F \cup \set{\tau}) = \bigsqcup F$ for every $\tau \in S$ by maximality, so
$\bigsqcup F$ bounds $S$; and every bound of $S$ bounds $F$, so $\bigsqcup F$ is least.
\end{proof}

\input{spec/fig/graduality-join}

\begin{definition}[Abstraction]
\label{def:abstraction}
Abstraction $\alpha : \powersetplus{\TypeSet} \to \GTypeSet$ is defined by
$\alpha(S) = \bigsqcup S$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:least-approximant}).
\end{definition}

\noindent Every gradual type approximates the empty set, and $\GTypeSet$ has no least element under
$\precTy$, so the empty set is excluded from the domain of $\alpha$. Concretisation and abstraction
form a \emph{Galois insertion}: $\alpha(S) \precTy \gty{\tau}$ if and only if
$S \subseteq \gamma(\gty{\tau})$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:least-approximant}), and
$\alpha(\gamma(\gty{\tau})) = \gty{\tau}$ by reflection
(Lemma~\ref{lem:precision-concretisation}).

A relation on types lifts to gradual types through concretisation \citep{garcia2016}: the lifted
relation holds when the concretisations contain a related pair. A conjunction of such statements
lifts in the same way, with one witness for each metavariable; when the conjuncts share no
metavariable the existentials factor, and the lifting is the conjunction of the lifted conjuncts.
A shared metavariable makes the conjunction a relational composition, with the middle witness
drawn from a concretisation rather than from all types; that the lifting of a composition is
stronger than the composition of the liftings is standard \citep{garcia2016}. Subtyping and
equivalence on types are as defined in \specification; equivalence lifts to \emph{consistent
equivalence} \citep{sekiyama2019}.

\begin{definition}[Consistent equivalence]
\label{def:consistent-equivalence}
$\Sigma \vdash \gty{\sigma} \gtyEquiv \gty{\tau}$ if and only if $\Sigma \vdash \sigma \tyEquiv \tau$ for some
$\sigma \in \gamma(\gty{\sigma})$ and $\tau \in \gamma(\gty{\tau})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Consistent three-way supertype and equivalence relations]
\label{def:three-sup-equiv}
$\Sigma \vdash \threeSup{\gty{\tau}}{\gty{\sigma}}{\gty{\tau}'}$ if and only if
$\Sigma \vdash \sigma \subty \tau$ and $\Sigma \vdash \sigma \subty \tau'$ for some
$\tau \in \gamma(\gty{\tau})$, $\tau' \in \gamma(\gty{\tau}')$ and
$\sigma \in \gamma(\gty{\sigma})$; likewise
$\Sigma \vdash \threeEquiv{\gty{\tau}}{\gty{\sigma}}{\gty{\tau}'}$, over equivalence.
\end{definition}

\figref{graduality-subtyping} defines \emph{consistent subtyping}, mutually with the consistent
three-way subtype relation of \figref{graduality-three-sub}, by lifting the subtyping rules of
\specification: types become gradual types, subtyping premises become consistent subtyping,
equivalence premises become consistent equivalence, and two axioms place $\tyAny$ below and above every
gradual type. The two premises of \ruleName{gsubty-union} share $\gty{\sigma}$ and are
replaced by the three-way premise; lifted separately they would derive
$\Sigma \vdash \tyUnion{\tyList{\tyInt}}{\tyList{\tyStr}} \gsubty \tyList{\tyAny}$, since
$\tyInt \gtyEquiv \tyAny$ and $\tyStr \gtyEquiv \tyAny$, although
$\gamma(\tyList{\tyAny}) = \set{\tyList{\tau} \mid \tau \in \TypeSet}$, and
$\Sigma \vdash \tyUnion{\tyList{\tyInt}}{\tyList{\tyStr}} \subty \tyList{\tau}$ requires
$\tyInt \tyEquiv \tau$ and $\tyStr \tyEquiv \tau$.

\input{spec/fig/graduality-subtyping}

\begin{lemma}[Correctness of consistent subtyping]
\label{lem:consistent-subtyping-correct}
$\Sigma \vdash \gty{\sigma} \gsubty \gty{\tau}$ if and only if
$\Sigma \vdash \sigma \subty \tau$ for some $\sigma \in \gamma(\gty{\sigma})$ and
$\tau \in \gamma(\gty{\tau})$.
\end{lemma}

\input{spec/fig/graduality-three-sub}

\begin{lemma}[Correctness of the three-way subtype relation]
\label{lem:three-sub-correct}
$\Sigma \vdash \threeSub{\gty{\tau}}{\gty{\sigma}}{\gty{\tau}'}$ if and only if
$\Sigma \vdash \tau \subty \sigma$ and $\Sigma \vdash \tau' \subty \sigma$ for some
$\tau \in \gamma(\gty{\tau})$, $\tau' \in \gamma(\gty{\tau}')$ and
$\sigma \in \gamma(\gty{\sigma})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By induction on the triple, ordered lexicographically by the total size of the three types and
then by the size of the first; cases on the triple, where each case matches one rule and is an equivalence. An outer
type $\tyAny$ or $\tyNever$ takes the witness $\tyNever$, leaving a consistent subtyping check on
the other outer type (\ruleName{three-sub-any-left}, \ruleName{three-sub-never-left}). A union in
an outer position reassociates, because its witness is a union, below a type exactly when both
components are (\ruleName{three-sub-assoc}). Otherwise each outer witness shares its
head with the gradual type it witnesses, and neither is a union. In the middle, $\tyAny$ admits the witness $\tyObject$
(\ruleName{three-sub-any}). A static middle type is its own sole concretisation, so sharing is
trivial and two consistent subtyping checks remain (\ruleName{three-sub-static}). For a union in
the middle, inversion sends each outer witness into one component: the same component
(\ruleName{three-sub-union-left}, \ruleName{three-sub-union-right}), or different components, in
which case the witnesses no longer interact (\ruleName{three-sub-split}). A constructor in the
middle must head both outer types, or the case refutes. Tuples proceed componentwise
(\ruleName{three-sub-tuple}); lists and dictionaries need one element witness equivalent to both
element types (\ruleName{three-sub-list}, \ruleName{three-sub-dict}), and callable arguments one
witness below both argument types (\ruleName{three-sub-callable}), the relations of
Definition~\ref{def:three-sup-equiv}. Each appeal to the hypothesis decreases the order.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rules for $\threeSup{\gty{\tau}}{\gty{\sigma}}{\gty{\tau}'}$}

The rules of \figref{graduality-three-sub} take the supertype relation as premises at callable
arguments. \figref{graduality-three-sup} gives rules for it by the case analysis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-sub-correct}, with the subtyping constraints reversed; every case
closes except one.

\begin{lemma}[Soundness of the supertype rules]
\label{lem:three-sup-sound}
The rules of \figref{graduality-three-sup} derive only instances of the consistent three-way
supertype relation.
\end{lemma}

\input{spec/fig/graduality-three-sup}

The residual case is a middle union $\tyUnion{\gty{\sigma}}{\gty{\sigma}'}$ that is not static.
Its witness is a union, below a type exactly when both components are, so the two conjuncts become
four constraints, relating each component witness to each outer witness; every witness now appears
in two constraints. The case amounts to the two judgements
$\threeSub{\gty{\sigma}}{\gty{\tau}}{\gty{\sigma}'}$ and
$\threeSub{\gty{\sigma}}{\gty{\tau}'}{\gty{\sigma}'}$ with the witnesses for $\gty{\sigma}$ and
$\gty{\sigma}'$ shared between them, a cycle of four constraints, which no one judgement
expresses. Splitting the union instead, with one judgement per component, is unsound:
$\threeSup{\tyList{\tyAny}}{\tyList{\tyInt}}{\tyList{\tyAny}}$ and
$\threeSup{\tyList{\tyAny}}{\tyList{\tyStr}}{\tyList{\tyAny}}$ hold, but
$\threeSup{\tyList{\tyAny}}{\tyUnion{\tyList{\tyInt}}{\tyList{\tyStr}}}{\tyList{\tyAny}}$ needs
an element witness equivalent to both $\tyInt$ and $\tyStr$.

Sharing the pair of component witnesses suggests a judgement over four gradual types, but the
widening does not stop there: a union in an outer position then sends each component witness below
one of its components independently, so the constraints no longer relate every pair. The general
form is a finite set of subtyping and equivalence constraints over shared witnesses, a constraint
system in the sense of \citet{garcia2015}. Its treatment is deferred, with rules for the
equivalence relation and decidability of the three relations.

\section{Constraints}
\label{sec:constraints}

The rules of \figref{graduality-three-sub} are incomplete:
$\threeSub{\tyUnion{\tyList{\tyInt}}{\tyList{\tyInt}}}{\tyList{\tyAny}}{\tyList{\tyInt}}$
satisfies the lifting of its two conjuncts but has no derivation, because reassociation cannot
reduce three list-headed components to two, so the completeness direction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-sub-correct} fails. With the residual case of the supertype relation, this
motivates constraints over shared witnesses in place of judgements of fixed arity.

\begin{definition}[Constraint system]
\label{def:constraint-system}
Let $x$, $y$ and $z$ range over constraint variables. A constraint system $\consys$ declares
finitely many variables, each with a gradual type as its range, and imposes finitely many
constraints $x \subty y$ and $x \tyEquiv y$ whose operands are declared variables or types.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Satisfaction]
\label{def:satisfaction}
$\Sigma \vDash \consys$ if and only if some map $\theta$, sending each variable to a member of
the concretisation of its range and each type to itself, validates every constraint as the
corresponding static judgement.
\end{definition}

\noindent The lifted relations are the small instances:
$\Sigma \vdash \threeSub{\gty{\tau}}{\gty{\sigma}}{\gty{\tau}'}$ if and only if
$\Sigma \vDash \langle x : \gty{\tau}, y : \gty{\tau}', z : \gty{\sigma} \mathrel{;}
x \subty z, y \subty z \rangle$, and consistent subtyping is the one-constraint case.

The solver rewrites a system; every step preserves and reflects satisfiability, and a choice is a
disjunction, so $\consys$ is satisfiable exactly when some run succeeds.
%
\begin{itemize}
\item A variable with static range is replaced by its range and discarded.
\item A constraint between types is checked by the static judgement, the run failing if the check
  does; a constraint with upper operand $\tyObject$ is deleted.
\item A variable with union range splits into fresh variables over the components: as lower
  operand, one constraint per component; as upper operand, one chosen component, provided the
  root of the other operand, or of its range, is neither $\tyAny$ nor a union. A union type as
  lower operand splits likewise.
\item Variables whose ranges share a root constructor unfold once, the argument positions
  receiving fresh variables shared by every constraint on the variable; constraints between
  unfolded operands decompose componentwise, into equivalence for list and dictionary elements,
  subtyping for tuple components and callable results, and reversed subtyping for callable
  arguments. Distinct root constructors fail.
\item A variable with range $\tyAny$ is an \emph{unknown}; when every remaining constraint
  relates unknowns to types alone, an unknown is assignable exactly when the join of its lower
  bounds lies below the meet of its upper bounds, by the lattice of \specification.
\end{itemize}
%
Deferred: constraints between two unknowns, unknowns against constructor operands, equivalence
constraints on split unions, and the proof that the solver succeeds exactly on satisfiable
systems.
